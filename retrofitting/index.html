Plan the details

Work with the home you have.

Investigate access before promising concealment

Wall construction, framing, and available attic or crawlspace access affect cable routes. Photos are a useful start, but a visit helps establish the practical options. Concealed wiring and particular mounting positions depend on what the property allows.

Work with surfaces and built-ins

Stone, timber, cabinetry, and existing openings can shape the installation. Review display weight, mounting conditions, and speaker depth alongside the appearance you want. Discuss alternative positions if the preferred route would require changes to finishes.

Connect an upgrade to the existing system

List the components, sources, and controls already in use. Compatibility can determine whether a new display, streaming source, or speaker upgrade needs additional changes. Understanding the connections first helps define a manageable scope.

Keep the result accessible

An upgrade should leave room to reach cables, ventilate equipment, and identify connections. Consider the controls the household will use and how future service access can be maintained after the equipment is installed.

Before we visit

Bring the details that shape the work. A useful first conversation starts with the property and the work already planned.

  • Room photographs and preferred positions
  • Known cable routes and access points
  • Equipment brands and model numbers
  • Finishes or built-ins that must be considered

Questions

Common questions

Will a retrofit require opening walls?

That depends on the room, wiring, and access. We review the installation conditions first, then discuss cable routes and any changes needed — we will not promise concealment before investigating.

Can you add a theater or speakers to a home that is already finished?

Yes — most of our work is exactly that. Wireless, architectural, and carefully routed wired options mean new displays, speakers, and controls rarely require tearing the home apart.

What do you need from us before visiting?

Room photographs and preferred positions, any known cable routes and access points, equipment brands and model numbers, and finishes or built-ins that must be considered.

Can you work with stone, timber, or existing built-ins?

Yes. We review display weight, mounting conditions, and speaker depth alongside the appearance you want, and discuss alternative positions where the preferred route would harm a finish.

Will you connect the new equipment to what we already have?

That is the plan. List the components, sources, and controls in use so we can check compatibility and define a manageable scope before anything is installed.

Let’s talk through your plans.

Send room photos and tell us what you want to add. HES will assess the access and recommend a practical way forward.

A good starting point

Every window is different.

HES works with Lutron shade solutions, including Palladiom wire-free options. Whether you call them automated shades or motorized blinds, measurements, fabric, and power belong in the same conversation. A useful start:

  • Windows that need privacy or glare control
  • Mounting space and existing trim
  • Wired power or battery access

Choose what the light does

Light-filtering fabric softens daylight while preserving a sense of the outdoors. Room-darkening options serve a different purpose. Think about screen glare, sleeping spaces, and privacy before selecting a fabric.

Make the groups useful

You may want one window to move independently and a wall of windows to move together. Decide which views, rooms, and times of day belong in a shared control group.

Plan for the long term

Wired and battery-powered shades bring different installation and maintenance needs. We review access, compatible controls, and any planned renovation before selecting the approach.

Discuss your shades

Share which windows glare, which need privacy, and what the trim and power look like. We’ll plan shades that suit the room and the long term.

Questions

Common questions

What is the difference between automated shades and motorized blinds?

Mostly wording. Both describe window coverings that move by motor rather than by hand — what matters is fabric, fit, power, and how you want to control them.

Do automated shades need to be wired?

Not necessarily. HES works with Lutron shade solutions including wire-free options that run on batteries, as well as wired shades. We review access, maintenance, and any planned renovation before recommending an approach.

Can shades be grouped by room or time of day?

Yes. You can move one window independently and a wall of windows together, or schedule groups around morning light, evening privacy, and screen glare.

Will they work with my existing smart home controls?

We review compatible controls before specifying anything. Shades can share controls with lighting and entertainment when the products and installation conditions support it.

Do motorized shades help with glare on a TV screen?

Absolutely — glare control is one of the most common reasons homeowners call. Light-filtering or room-darkening fabric, placed on the right windows, makes daytime viewing far more comfortable.

Which windows would you like to change?

Tell us about the glare, the privacy, and the hard-to-reach glass. HES will plan the shades, the fabric, and the controls around your home.
